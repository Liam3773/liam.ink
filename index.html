Chapter 9 — The Target Market

Media task model answer: Summarise the unseen article under Ethics and sustainability, Business and financial literacy, Entrepreneurial thinking and Digital transformation. For each heading, state what happened, why it mattered, and how it affected customers, costs, growth, reputation or technology.
1. The five product life cycle stages are development, introduction, growth, maturity and decline.
2. Short life cycle: fidget spinners. Medium life cycle: a smartphone model. Long life cycle: a staple food brand or essential service.
3. Market research is the gathering and analysing of information about customers, competitors and market trends. A target market is the specific group of customers a business aims its product or service at.
4. Market research reduces risk, helps identify customer needs and guides decisions on product, price, place and promotion.
5. Field research is first-hand primary research collected by the business. Desk research is secondary research using information already available.
6. Focus group: field. Observation in a marketplace: field. Magazines and newspapers: desk. Direct interview survey: field. CSO publications: desk.
7. Primary research methods include surveys and focus groups. Secondary research methods include CSO statistics and published reports/articles.
8. A focus group is a small group of target customers asked for opinions. Test marketing is launching a product in a limited area before a full launch.
9. Market segmentation means dividing a broad market into smaller groups of customers with similar needs or characteristics.
10. Three bases of segmentation are demographic, geographic and psychographic/behavioural. Examples include teenage skincare, winter clothes in colder regions and premium fitness products for health-conscious consumers.
11. A niche market is a small, specialised market with specific customer needs.
12. The marketing mix helps a business combine product, price, place and promotion to satisfy its target market.
13. Product is what is sold; price is what is charged; place is how it reaches customers; promotion is how customers are informed and persuaded.
14. Packaging factors: expensive necklace—security, protection and premium image; handmade pottery—cushioning, fragile handling and eco-friendly look; Easter eggs—seasonal design, food labelling and protection; soft drink can—convenience, branding and recycling information; meat products—hygiene, expiry date and temperature safety.
15. USP means Unique Selling Proposition: the feature that makes a product different from competitors.
16. Branding means creating a clear identity through name, logo, design, quality and reputation. Examples include Apple and Nike.
17. Pricing factors include costs, competitor prices and customer willingness to pay.
18. Premium pricing means high price for prestige. Skimming means high launch price then reducing it. Penetration pricing means low launch price to gain market share. Economy pricing means low cost and low price. Dynamic pricing changes with demand, time or data.
19. Rolex: premium pricing. Lidl milk below cost: loss leader. Spotify student discount: promotional/segmented pricing. Apple new iPhone: skimming.
20. Distribution channels include direct producer to consumer, producer to retailer to consumer, producer to wholesaler to retailer to consumer, and producer to agent/platform to consumer.
21. Chocolate bars: coupons, multipacks or in-store displays. Streaming app: free trial, referral offer or student discount. Nail bar: loyalty card, package deal or giveaway.
22. Loyalty programmes benefit customers through rewards and savings. Retailers benefit through repeat business, customer data and retention.
23. An ethical concern with loyalty programmes is customer data privacy and possible manipulation of buying behaviour.
24. Public relations methods include press releases, sponsorship and community/CSR events.
25. Sponsorship examples include Adidas sponsoring athletes, Red Bull sponsoring Formula 1/extreme sports and a local bank or retailer sponsoring a GAA club.
26. Advertising categories include informative, persuasive, reminder and comparative advertising.
27. The target market influences product features, price level, distribution method and promotional message. A premium adult target market usually needs premium design, selective distribution and polished promotion.
28. ASA complaints: complaint is submitted, evidence is checked, the advertisement is assessed against the code, a ruling may be made, the ad may be changed or withdrawn, and the ruling may be published.
29. The Green Claims Directive aims to make environmental claims clear, evidence-based and verifiable. It should reduce greenwashing but may increase compliance costs.
30. Social media is a marketing and research tool because it allows targeting, communication, engagement tracking and real-time customer feedback.
31. Algorithms push content to likely audiences. Cookies track user behaviour for targeting and analytics. Search engine marketing reaches customers when they search for related products.
32. Gamification means using points, badges, rewards, streaks or leaderboards to increase engagement.
33. STEEPLE helps analyse external factors before making business decisions.
34. STEEPLE means Social, Technological, Economic, Environmental, Political, Legal and Ethical.
35. Example STEEPLE for a coffee shop expansion: social demand, digital ordering, inflation, waste rules, planning permission, food/employment law and ethical sourcing.

Chapter 10 — Operations and Finance

Media task model answer: Link the unseen article/podcast to operational efficiency, finance choice, growth opportunity and digital systems under the four cross-cutting themes.
1. Bakery operational model: inputs such as ingredients and labour are transformed using equipment and processes into bread/cakes, with quality control and distribution to customers.
2. Just-in-time means ordering raw materials only when needed to reduce storage costs and waste.
3. Supply chain management is managing the flow of materials, information and finished goods from suppliers to customers.
4. Monitoring operations helps reduce waste, control costs, improve quality and respond quickly to problems.
5. Finance factors: amount required, purpose, term, cost, security/collateral, control and cash-flow impact.
6. Short-term finance: overdraft, trade credit, credit card. Medium-term: leasing, hire purchase, medium-term bank loan. Long-term: mortgage, debenture, equity/share capital.
7. Overdraft is a bank-approved negative balance. Credit card allows buy now and pay later. Trade credit is delayed supplier payment. Factoring is selling invoices for immediate cash.
8. Leasing is renting and returning the vehicle. HP involves instalments and ownership at the end. PCP has lower instalments with an optional final balloon payment. Leasing has lower upfront costs but no ownership; HP gives ownership but higher repayments; PCP gives lower monthly payments but may include mileage limits and a large final payment.
9. Retained earnings are profits kept in the business. Advantage: no interest or loss of control. Disadvantage: limited amount and reduced dividends.
10. Crowdfunding means raising small amounts from many people through a platform. Types include reward-based and equity crowdfunding.
11. Examples of platforms include Kickstarter, GoFundMe, Crowdcube and Seedrs.
12. Venture capital is equity finance provided to high-growth businesses in return for ownership and a share of future returns.
13. An angel investor is an individual who invests personal money in an early-stage business.
14. Angel investment is usually smaller and earlier-stage personal funding. Venture capital is larger professional fund investment.
15. A PLC can raise equity by selling shares to investors. Advantage: no fixed repayment. Disadvantage: ownership and control are diluted.
16. A debenture is a long-term company loan certificate on which interest is paid.
17. A loan is borrowed money that must be repaid. A grant is usually non-repayable funding if conditions are met.
18. Fixed costs stay constant in the short run; variable costs change with output. Restaurant fixed costs: rent, insurance, manager salary. Variable: ingredients, hourly staff, packaging. Clothes shop fixed: rent, rates, insurance. Variable: stock, bags, card fees. Online retailer fixed: website costs, warehouse rent, insurance. Variable: inventory, postage, packaging.
19. Variable costs = €2,500. Fixed costs = €1,300. Total costs = €3,800.
20. Business rates are local-authority charges on commercial property.
21. Revenue = price × quantity sold. Profit = revenue − total costs.
22. Business B revenue = 150 × €8 = €1,200. Profit = €1,000.
23. Revenue streams include sales, subscriptions, commission, rent, service fees and advertising.
24. A cashflow forecast predicts future cash receipts and payments. It helps avoid shortages and plan borrowing or investment.
25. Net cash surplus means receipts exceed payments. Net cash deficit means payments exceed receipts.
26. Opening cash is cash at the start of a period. Closing cash is cash at the end.
27. Insolvency means a business cannot pay its debts as they fall due or its liabilities exceed its assets.
28. Guesthouse: February net cash = €3,000. March is better than January because March has positive net cash and a positive closing balance. To increase January receipts, use off-season promotions or stronger online marketing.
29. Golf club receipts could include membership fees and green fees. Payments could include wages and course maintenance. March is better than January because March has positive net cash and closing cash while January is negative.

Chapter 11 — Growth, Development and Expansion

Media task model answer: Group the unseen article under the four cross-cutting themes and evaluate whether the expansion looked viable.
1. Competition means rivalry between firms selling similar goods or services to the same customers.
2. Porter’s five forces are rivalry, threat of new entrants, threat of substitutes, supplier power and buyer power.
3. Porter’s model examines how these forces affect industry attractiveness and profitability.
4. Netflix: rivalry is high; substitutes include gaming/social media; supplier power is high for premium content; buyer power is moderate because switching is easy; entry barriers are medium-high due to content cost and scale.
5. Barriers to entry stop new firms entering. Barriers to exit make it difficult or expensive to leave an industry.
6. A substitute is an alternative product meeting the same need, e.g. streaming substituting for cinema or cable TV.
7. Supplier power is the strength suppliers have over prices and terms. Buyer power is the strength customers have over prices, choice and switching.
8. Competitive advantage is a feature giving a business an edge, such as lower costs, stronger brand or better technology.
9. Reasons to expand include higher profits, economies of scale, risk spreading, bigger market share and stronger bargaining power.
10. Diversification means entering a new product or market area. Example: Amazon moving from books into cloud, devices and streaming.
11. Vertical backward integration means taking over a supplier. Vertical forward integration means taking over a distributor or retailer.
12. Organic expansion is internal growth, such as opening a branch. Inorganic expansion is external growth, such as merger, takeover or alliance.
13. Recent merger example: Vodafone UK and Three UK merged to form VodafoneThree.
14. Recent takeover example: Microsoft acquired Activision Blizzard.
15. Strategic alliance example: Starbucks and PepsiCo’s ready-to-drink coffee partnership.
16. Technology supports expansion through e-commerce, CRM, data analytics, automation, cloud systems and digital marketing.
17. CRM stands for Customer Relationship Management. It tracks interactions and helps personalise service and marketing.
18. A business owner uses cost-benefit analysis to check whether expansion is financially worthwhile.
19. Three steps: identify costs, identify benefits and compare/evaluate the net result.
20. Aldi expansion CBA: costs include fit-out, staff, stock and marketing. Benefits include higher sales, market share and economies of scale. Expansion should happen only if expected benefits exceed costs and risk.

Chapter 12 — Managing Risk

1. Risk is the possibility that an event will cause loss or harm to a business. Examples include supply failure, cyberattack and reputational damage.
2. Operational risks include machinery breakdown and supply disruption.
3. Competitive risks: airline—price wars and route competition; clothes manufacturer—fast-fashion rivals and changing trends; hotel—discount rivals and Airbnb; streaming platform—free tiers and exclusive-content rivals.
4. Example: Volkswagen’s emissions scandal damaged trust, reputation and finances.
5. Economic risks: car dealership—higher interest rates and weaker demand; jewellery store—lower discretionary spending and higher input prices; construction company—higher material costs and rate-sensitive demand; food franchise—energy/food inflation and consumer cutbacks.
6. Brexit created customs, currency and trade uncertainty for Irish businesses. A protectionist foreign election can hurt Irish exporters through tariffs or policy uncertainty.
7. Operational risk relates to internal processes. Economic risk relates to the economy. Geopolitical risk comes from political events. Competitive risk comes from rivals. Financial risk relates to cash, credit and interest rates. Cybersecurity risk is digital attack or data loss. Reputational risk damages public image. Environmental risk relates to climate or pollution. Compliance risk means breaking laws or regulations.
8. Car manufacturer: diversify suppliers, quality control, contingency stock. Luxury handbag retailer: anti-counterfeit systems, cybersecurity, inventory planning. Bakery: food safety, maintenance, backup suppliers/insurance. Construction company: safety procedures, project risk assessment, contingency budget. Fintech company: cybersecurity, AML/compliance controls, disaster recovery.
9. Insurance: restaurant—public liability/business interruption; gym—public liability; ice rink—public liability; farm—farm/property/crop cover; retailer—stock/property insurance; outdoor activity centre—public liability.

Chapter 13 — Leading and Managing an Organisation

1. A manager plans, organises, directs and controls resources to achieve objectives.
2. A leader sets a vision, inspires people, motivates change and builds commitment.
3. Autocratic leadership is fast and clear but can demotivate staff. Democratic leadership encourages ideas and commitment but can be slow. Participative leadership involves employees strongly in decisions but can take time.
4. Delegation means giving authority and responsibility to another person. Employee benefit: trust and skill development. Manager benefit: more time for strategic work.
5. Organisational culture is the shared values, norms and behaviours in a workplace.
6. Four culture types: power culture, role culture, task culture and person culture.
7. Toxic culture is a workplace where fear, blame, bullying, unfairness or mistrust are normal. Causes include poor leadership, weak communication, favouritism, no accountability and unrealistic pressure.
8. Causes of conflict: poor communication, personality clashes, unclear roles, unfair treatment and competition for scarce resources.
9. Conflict may cause low morale, lower productivity, absenteeism, mistakes and staff turnover.
10. Equal opportunities means fair access to jobs, training and promotion based on merit, not discrimination. Example: rejecting a qualified person because of disability, gender or race.
11. Staff turnover is the rate at which employees leave and are replaced. Causes include poor pay, poor leadership, limited progression, stress and poor culture.
12. Internal conflict solutions include negotiation and grievance procedures. External solutions include WRC mediation/conciliation and Labour Court or arbitration referral.
13. Mediation uses a neutral third party to help agreement. Conciliation is a structured facilitated process, often in industrial relations. Arbitration gives an independent decision that parties may accept as binding.
14. The WRC provides information, inspection/enforcement, mediation, conciliation and adjudication of employment and equality disputes.
15. The Labour Court hears employment-rights appeals and acts as a court of last resort in industrial relations disputes.
16. The WRC usually handles first-instance complaints and workplace-resolution services. The Labour Court mainly handles appeals and unresolved industrial-relations matters.
17. In Equal Status cases, the WRC receives the complaint, may offer mediation, may hold an adjudication hearing and may order redress if discrimination is proven.

Chapter 14 — Leading and Managing People

Media task model answer: Link the unseen article to recruitment fairness, wellbeing, motivation, employee development and digital working practices.
1. Workforce planning means ensuring the organisation has the right number of people, with the right skills, in the right jobs, at the right time.
2. Internal factors: strategy, budget, turnover, skills gaps and automation. External factors: labour supply, demographics, law, competition and technology.
3. Workforce audit elements include employee numbers, skills/qualifications and age/experience profile.
4. A virtual assistant is a remote worker or software-enabled service that performs administrative or customer-support tasks digitally.
5. Succession planning means preparing people to fill important roles in the future.
6. Alternatives to filling vacancies include overtime, temporary staff, outsourcing and upskilling current staff.
7. Recruitment process outsourcing means using an external specialist to manage some or all recruitment activities.
8. Internal recruitment fills vacancies from existing employees. External recruitment searches outside the organisation.
9. Recruitment stages: plan the vacancy, attract applicants, select and appoint the best candidate.
10. A job description lists duties and responsibilities. A person specification lists the skills, qualities and qualifications required.
11. Screening removes unsuitable applications. Shortlisting selects the strongest candidates for interview.
12. Interview factors include structured questions, trained/fair panel, clear scoring and accessibility arrangements.
13. Unconscious bias means hidden automatic assumptions that influence decisions without the person realising.
14. Types of unconscious bias include affinity bias, halo effect and confirmation bias.
15. Ethical recruitment uses transparent criteria, equal treatment, objective assessment, reasonable accommodation and honest job adverts.
16. Equality, diversity and inclusion widen the talent pool, reduce legal risk, support fairness and improve innovation.
17. Employers can be sensitive to applicant wellbeing by communicating clearly, scheduling reasonably and providing accessible processes or adjustments.
18. Motivation is the willingness to put effort into work to achieve goals.
19. Intrinsic motivation comes from internal satisfaction. Extrinsic motivation comes from external rewards.
20. Financial rewards include wages/salary, bonuses, commission and profit-sharing.
21. Profit-sharing can motivate employees because they benefit directly when the business performs well.
22. Effective appraisal includes objective setting, evidence-based review, two-way discussion, feedback and an action plan.
23. Self-appraisal means employees reflect on their own performance. Summative appraisal is an end-of-period review. Formative appraisal gives ongoing feedback. 360-degree feedback gathers views from multiple sources.
24. These appraisal methods can motivate by improving clarity, recognition, development and fairness.
25. Human capital management means treating employees’ skills, knowledge and experience as valuable assets to be developed.
26. The organisational environment affects human capital through culture, leadership, resources, strategy and technology.
27. On-the-job training happens while doing the work. Off-the-job training happens away from normal duties.
28. Induction elements include company overview, policies, role expectations, health and safety, introductions and initial training.
29. CPD means continuous professional development. Examples include short courses, workshops, webinars, mentoring and professional reading.
30. CPD benefits employees through better skills, stronger confidence and improved career progression.
31. Tuckman’s team stages are forming, storming, norming, performing and adjourning.
32. For employees, teamwork can improve support, learning and motivation but may create conflict or free-riding. For employers, teams can improve problem-solving and flexibility but may slow decisions.
33. Remote working is mainly off-site. Blended/hybrid working combines remote and on-site work.
34. One conflict area is disagreement over attendance expectations, monitoring or availability.
35. Employees gain flexibility and less commuting but may face isolation and blurred boundaries. Employers gain wider talent access and space savings but may face communication, culture and supervision challenges.
36. Work-life balance means maintaining a healthy relationship between work demands and personal life.
37. New working patterns can improve balance through flexibility but harm it through “always on” expectations.
38. Corporate wellness means organised employer efforts to support physical, mental and social wellbeing at work.
39. Measures include EAP/counselling, flexible working, health initiatives, wellbeing days and training.
40. Good wellness practices can increase motivation, trust and retention and create a supportive culture.
41. Leadership is crucial because leaders set the tone, allocate resources and model healthy behaviours.

Chapter 18 — Making Informed Financial Decisions

Media task model answer: Summarise the unseen article under the four cross-cutting headings and connect the points to saving, borrowing, financial risk and digital change.
1. Saving: interest rate, access, inflation and safety. Investing: risk, return, time horizon and diversification. Borrowing: APR, affordability, term, total repayable and missed-payment consequences.
2. Credit union: savings/share account, loans and current-account-style services where offered. Fintech such as Revolut: payments/card, transfers/FX and savings/investing features. Bank: current account, mortgage and credit card.
3. The Deposit Guarantee Scheme protects eligible deposits up to €100,000 per depositor per institution if a participating bank, building society or credit union fails.
4. DIRT is tax deducted from deposit interest. Example: if gross interest is €100 and DIRT is 33%, the saver receives €67.
5. AER means Annual Equivalent Rate and compares savings returns. APR means Annual Percentage Rate and compares borrowing costs.
6. A demand account gives easy access to savings. A notice account requires notice before withdrawal.
7. Interest should ideally equal or exceed inflation so savings do not lose real purchasing power.
8. Five investment options are shares, bonds, property, diversified funds/ETFs and pension contributions.
9. Risk tolerance is the level of risk and possible loss a consumer is willing and able to accept.
10. Stock market risk: price volatility. Small crypto risk: collapse or illiquidity. Property risk: falling values or difficulty selling quickly.
11. Reliable financial information sources include the CCPC, Central Bank of Ireland, Revenue, regulated providers’ official documents and regulated financial advisers.
12. Reasons to switch: current account—lower fees or better digital features; mortgage—lower interest and repayments; demand account—better savings rate.
13. Fintech means using technology to deliver financial services.
14. A neobank is a digital-only bank with no traditional branch network.
15. Fintech is disruptive because it changes how financial services are delivered and pressures traditional firms on speed, cost and user experience.
16. Benefits of fintech include convenience and lower-cost innovation. Challenges include cyber risk and exclusion of less digital users.
17. Revolut-style model: provide app-based cards/payments at scale, then earn from interchange, subscriptions, FX and extra services.
18. Creditworthiness is assessed using income, existing debt, repayment history, affordability and credit-report information. Arrears, high debt and repeated applications can damage credit rating. Poor credit may lead to rejection, smaller limits or higher borrowing costs.
19. The regulator is the Central Bank of Ireland. It regulates firms, protects consumers and monitors financial stability. FSPO means Financial Services and Pensions Ombudsman; it provides independent complaints resolution for financial and pension services.
20. Under-regulation can cause excessive risky lending, asset bubbles, bank failures, taxpayer bailouts, recession and loss of trust, as shown by the 2008 financial crash.
21. Online fraud examples include phishing/smishing and fake investment scams.
22. A money mule is a person who allows their bank account to be used to move stolen or illegal money.
23. Crypto risks include extreme volatility, scams/theft and limited consumer protection.
24. CBDC means Central Bank Digital Currency. The proposed digital euro would be a digital form of central-bank money that complements cash and is offered through supervised intermediaries. Impacts may include easier euro-area payments and more consumer choice, but also adjustment costs for banks and merchants. Difference: a CBDC is issued by a central bank, while a stablecoin is privately issued and depends on reserves and issuer strength.
25. Mortgage risks: interest-rate rises and arrears/negative equity. Cryptocurrency risks: price collapse and fraud/theft. Pension risks: investment loss and inflation or insufficient retirement income.
